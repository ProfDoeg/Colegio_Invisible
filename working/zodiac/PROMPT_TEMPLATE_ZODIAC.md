# The zodiac dossier prompt

Anthony's request, 2026-09-12: "As a separate research I'd like 12 research dossiers on each
of the 12 figures of the zodiac. We can keep that in a folder called zodiac." Separate from
the atlas: no roster, no journey. The person-dossier prompt (dossiers/PROMPT_TEMPLATE.md)
rewritten for a sky figure. `[NAME]` is the only substitution point; the per-sign brief follows.

---

[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this figure of the WESTERN
zodiac: the Babylonian, Egyptian, Greek, Roman, Arabic, Persian, Latin and modern European
lineage of the twelve signs. I want the fullest reconstructable history of the figure, from its
earliest attestation to the present: the stars, the picture, the myths, the names, the doctrines
and the uses, chronologically and in detail. Other zodiacs (Chinese, Mesoamerican) are out of
scope except where they touched the western one (the Indian rashi, which received it). Include,
wherever available:

- the names: Babylonian (Akkadian and Sumerian), Egyptian, Greek, Latin, Arabic, Persian,
  Hebrew, medieval Latin and the vernaculars, Spanish; the etymologies; the symbol (glyph) and
  its history
- the stars: which stars make the figure, the brightest and their proper names (with the
  Arabic, Greek and Latin derivations), the asterisms inside it, the boundary changes
  from Ptolemy to Bayer, Hevelius, Bode and the IAU; the ecliptic position, precession and
  the drift between sign and constellation; the age of Aries question; sidereal versus tropical
- the earliest attestations: MUL.APIN and the Babylonian star lists, the zodiac's formation in
  the fifth century BCE, the Egyptian decans and Dendera, Eudoxus and Aratus, Eratosthenes'
  catasterism, Hipparchus, Ptolemy's Almagest and Tetrabiblos, Manilius, Firmicus, Vettius
  Valens, Abu Ma'shar, al-Sufi, the Picatrix, the Indian rashi (Varahamihira, the
  Yavanajataka), the Persian and Arabic transmissions, the Latin translations of the twelfth
  century, Dante, the Renaissance (Schifanoia, Agrippa, Dürer's map, Bayer), Kepler's
  astrology, the Enlightenment atlases (Flamsteed, Bode), the Theosophists, twentieth-century
  astrology, and the present
- THE CLASSICAL MYTHS, EXHAUSTIVELY (the author's first priority): every Greek and Roman myth
  attached to the figure, not a selection. Every catasterism and every variant of it in
  Eratosthenes' Catasterismi and the Epitome, Hyginus' Poetica Astronomica and Fabulae, the
  scholia to Aratus and to Germanicus, Ovid's Fasti and Metamorphoses, Manilius' Astronomica,
  Nonnus, Pseudo-Apollodorus, Pausanias and the mythographers; who placed the figure in the sky,
  why, which god, which crime or gift; the competing identities (which ram, which bull, which
  twins, which archer: Crotus or Chiron, which fishes); and beyond the catasterisms every
  classical myth in which the figure's animal or person acts (the Golden Fleece, the Bull of
  Europa and of Marathon and of Crete, the Dioscuri's whole cycle, the Lernaean crab, the Nemean
  lion, Astraea and Erigone, the scales of Zeus and of Themis, Orion's scorpion, the centaurs,
  Pan and Typhon, Ganymede and Deucalion, Aphrodite and Eros at the Euphrates, and their kin),
  each with its earliest source, its date, and its later retellings. Give each myth its own
  paragraph with the ancient citations (author, work, book and section).
- the other myths: the Babylonian
  and Egyptian stories behind the same stars; the animal or person as it appears in scripture,
  hagiography, folklore, alchemy and Kabbalah (the tribe of Israel, the apostle in Julius
  Schiller's Coelum Stellatum Christianum of 1627, the Hebrew letter in the Sefer Yetzirah
  tradition, the alchemical operation assigned to the sign)
- the doctrine: element, modality, ruling planet (classical and modern), exaltation and fall,
  decans and their rulers, terms, the parts of the body, the seasons and the labors of the
  months, the medical and agricultural uses, the dates (tropical and sidereal), the
  characterology and its history
- iconography: the figure's depiction across manuscripts, ceilings, globes, atlases, coins,
  cathedral portals and calendars, with dates and places; the Bode plate; the Dendera relief;
  the Schifanoia fresco; the Très Riches Heures
- famous people, events and works attached to the sign or the constellation: births claimed,
  battles and foundings timed to it, poems, operas, paintings, buildings aligned to its stars
- controversies and disputes: contested star identifications, contested etymologies, the
  Ophiuchus "thirteenth sign" argument, the precession dispute, fabricated or misattributed
  ancient sources, modern hoaxes and popular errors

Include the good and the bad without judgment, and myth as fully as fact.
For every significant claim distinguish documented fact, scholarly reconstruction, tradition,
disputed claim, unverified claim, legend, myth and modern invention, and say which sources carry
it, when the story first appears, who spread it, and what evidence exists.
Do not dismiss a claim because it is popular or astrological; investigate its history.
Do not use fact-checking summaries as substitutes for the sources; trace to the texts.

Structure the dossier as: Basic identification (names, glyph, stars, dates); Stars and
boundaries; Chronology of attestation (dated, placed); Myths and legends by culture; Doctrine
and uses; Iconography (dated, placed); People, events and works; Controversies and disputes;
Open questions; Sources (full list of URLs and editions consulted).
Length: as long as the material requires; a thousand lines is not too many.
