[NAME]
Please conduct a comprehensive, source-driven research dossier on this tribe of Israel. I
want the fullest reconstructable account, from the eponymous son in Genesis to the tribe's
fate and afterlife in tradition, chronologically and in detail. Include, wherever available:

- THE NAME AND THE SON: the birth narrative and the folk etymology of the name (Genesis
  29-30, 35), the mother, the birth order; every episode in which the son acts in Genesis;
  the blessing of Jacob (Genesis 49) and the blessing of Moses (Deuteronomy 33) quoted in
  full in a standard translation with the Hebrew key words and their disputed readings
- HISTORY: the tribe in Exodus and Numbers (censuses, camp position, the standard and its

  Solomon, in the divided kingdom, in the Assyrian deportations of 732 and 722 BCE or the
  Babylonian exile, in Chronicles and the genealogies, in Ezekiel's ideal allotment

  the archaeology and historical-critical scholarship of the tribe (was it ever a tribe,
  amphictyony theories, the Merneptah stele, Mesha stele, the Song of Deborah's list)

  cities and sites that carry the name today
- SYMBOLS AND EMBLEMS: the animal or object of Jacob's blessing; the stone of the high
  priest's breastplate (Exodus 28, 39) in every assignment (Josephus, the Septuagint, the
  Vulgate, the rabbinic lists, modern gemology); the colour and device of the tribal
  banner in Midrash Bamidbar Rabbah 2:7 and the targumim; the emblems in the Chagall
  windows at Hadassah (1962) and other synagogue cycles; the modern Israeli use of tribal
  emblems (municipal seals, stamps, coins, the 1950s "twelve tribes" iconography); the
  tribe's month (Sefer Yetzirah's twelve simple letters, months and tribes), its direction
  of camp, its gate in Ezekiel
- THE ZODIAC OVERLAY, IN FULL: H. P. Blavatsky's correspondence of this tribe with a sign

  breastplate as the zodiac, Josephus Antiquities III.7.7, Philo); then EVERY other
  correspondence scheme: Josephus and Philo, the rabbinic and kabbalistic (the Zohar,
  Sefer Yetzirah commentaries, the Bahir), Athanasius Kircher's Oedipus Aegyptiacus (1652)
