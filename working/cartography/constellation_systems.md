# Constellation systems: a catalog

First pass, 2026-09-12, at Anthony's request ("Help me catalog the different constellation
systems"). Companion to `future_data_types.md`, idea 2 (constellations with their myths). Counts
are given as the tradition's own canonical figure where one exists; where scholars disagree the
range is stated. Atlas subjects who belong to a system are named with their slug; "not built"
marks a mapmaker who is not yet a subject. What is already inscribed on chain is noted last.

## 1. Mesopotamian (Babylonian and Assyrian)

- **Sources.** The star lists of the Kassite period (the "Three Stars Each" tablets, c. 1100 BCE),
  MUL.APIN (compiled c. 1000 BCE, copies from the 7th century), the astronomical diaries and
  zodiacal texts of the Persian and Seleucid periods.
- **Organizing principle.** The sky divided into three bands by the paths of Enlil (north), Anu
  (equator) and Ea (south); MUL.APIN lists some 66 to 71 named stars and constellations by path
  and by heliacal rising date. Around the 5th century BCE the twelve equal signs of the zodiac
  are cut from the ecliptic constellations: the ancestor of every later zodiac.
- **Figures.** The Bull of Heaven, the Scorpion, the Goat-fish (Capricornus), the Great Twins,
  the Furrow (Virgo's ear of grain), the Lion, Pabilsag the archer (Sagittarius), the Hired Man
  (Aries), the Field (the Square of Pegasus), the Wagon (the Plough).
- **Atlas.** nabucodonosor_ii (the diaries begin in his dynasty's world); no astronomer built.

## 2. Egyptian

- **Sources.** Coffin lids of the Middle Kingdom, the astronomical ceilings (Senenmut's tomb,
  c. 1470 BCE; the Ramesseum; the Dendera zodiac, 1st century BCE), the decan lists.
- **Organizing principle.** Thirty-six decans, ten-day star groups whose risings measured the
  hours of the night; a native northern sky (the Bull's Foreleg for the Plough, the Hippopotamus,
  the crocodile, the mooring post) unrelated to the Greek figures; Sah (Orion) and Sopdet (Sirius)
  as Osiris and Isis. The Dendera ceiling shows the Babylonian zodiac already fused with the
  decans and the Egyptian figures.
- **Atlas.** none built (Cleopatra's Dendera is the temple of her dynasty; a stop, not a system).

## 3. Greek and Ptolemaic (the classical 48)

- **Sources.** Eudoxus of Cnidus (4th century BCE, lost, known through Aratus), Aratus,
  Phaenomena (c. 275 BCE), Eratosthenes' Catasterisms (the myths of the figures), Hipparchus'
  commentary and catalogue (c. 130 BCE), Ptolemy, Almagest (c. 150 CE): 48 constellations,
  1,022 stars, the canon of the western sky for fourteen centuries.
- **Organizing principle.** Twenty-one northern, twelve zodiacal, fifteen southern figures, each
  a myth: the catasterism, a person or beast placed among the stars by a god. Ecliptic longitude
  and latitude.
- **Figures beyond the zodiac.** Ursa Major and Minor, Draco, Cepheus, Cassiopeia, Andromeda,
  Perseus, Pegasus, Auriga, Boötes, Hercules, Lyra, Cygnus, Aquila, Ophiuchus and Serpens,
  Corona Borealis, Sagitta, Delphinus, Equuleus, Triangulum; Orion, Canis Major and Minor, Lepus,
  Eridanus, Cetus, Argo Navis, Hydra, Crater, Corvus, Centaurus, Lupus, Ara, Corona Australis,
  Piscis Austrinus.
- **Atlas.** aristotle (the sphere of fixed stars in De caelo), plato (the Timaeus sky), virgil,
  cicero (his Aratea translation), hypatia (the Almagest commentaries at Alexandria); Ptolemy,
  Hipparchus, Aratus, Eratosthenes not built.

## 4. Arabic and Islamic

- **Two systems, not one.** (a) The Bedouin sky, pre-Islamic and folk: the anwa', star risings
  and settings that mark the seasons, and asterisms of camels, ostriches, a giant (al-Jawza,
  Orion), the daughters of the bier (the Plough's handle), Suhayl (Canopus) alone in the south.
  (b) The Ptolemaic sky received and corrected: al-Sufi, Book of the Fixed Stars (964 CE), the
  48 figures redrawn in Arab dress with the Bedouin names set beside the Greek ones; Ulugh Beg's
  Samarkand catalogue (1437).
- **The lunar mansions.** Twenty-eight manazil al-qamar, stations of the moon along the
  ecliptic, of Indian or Babylonian ancestry, used for the calendar and for astrology across the
  Islamic world and into medieval Europe (the Picatrix).
- **Atlas.** ibn_arabi and the Sufi files carry the sky as symbol; al-Sufi and Ulugh Beg not
  built. On chain: "Sky of al-Jawza" (root 4e53bb26...), al-Sufi's Orion, 62 stars in 10 groups,
  the corpus's only constellation-level inscription besides Bode's whole sky.

## 5. Indian

- **Sources.** The Vedas and the Vedanga Jyotisha (nakshatra lists), the Surya Siddhanta,
  Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita (6th century CE).
- **Organizing principle.** Twenty-seven (in older lists twenty-eight) nakshatras, lunar
  mansions of 13 degrees 20 minutes each, each with a ruling deity and a marker star (Krittika
  the Pleiades, Rohini Aldebaran, Chitra Spica); the twelve rashis of the zodiac arrive with
  Hellenistic astrology (the Yavanajataka, 2nd century CE). No independent set of pictorial
  constellations comparable to the Greek 48; the nakshatra is the unit.
- **Atlas.** none built.

## 6. Chinese

- **Sources.** The star catalogues of Shi Shen, Gan De and Wu Xian (4th century BCE, reconstructed
  through Chen Zhuo's 3rd-century CE synthesis), the Dunhuang star chart (c. 700 CE), the Suzhou
  planisphere (1247), Xu Guangqi's reform with the Jesuits (1630s).
- **Organizing principle.** Entirely independent of the Greek sky: three enclosures (the
  Purple Forbidden, the Supreme Palace, the Heavenly Market) around the pole, and twenty-eight
  lunar mansions (xiu) grouped by the four symbols (Azure Dragon, Vermilion Bird, White Tiger,
  Black Tortoise); 283 asterisms and 1,464 stars in the traditional count. The figures are a
  bureaucracy and a landscape, not myths: officers, kitchens, granaries, the celestial river.
- **Atlas.** matteo_ricci (the Jesuit sky meets the Chinese one); Xu Guangqi not built.

## 7. Mesoamerican

- **Maya.** The Paris Codex zodiac pages (a sequence of thirteen animals hung from a sky band:
  scorpion, turtle, rattlesnake, bat, bird, and others, the reading contested), the Pleiades as
  a rattlesnake's rattle, Orion's belt-and-sword triangle as the three hearthstones of creation,
  the Milky Way as the World Tree or the road to Xibalba. Not a fixed count.
- **Aztec.** Mamalhuaztli (the fire drill, Orion's belt), Tianquiztli (the marketplace, the
  Pleiades), Citlaltlachtli (the ballcourt), the Colotl scorpion; the New Fire ceremony timed
  by the Pleiades' zenith passage.
- **Atlas.** cortes and the Mexican files touch the calendar, none the sky as a system.

## 8. Andean (Inca)

- **Organizing principle.** Two kinds of constellation at once: star-to-star figures, and the
  dark cloud constellations, the yana phuyu, seen in the dust of the Milky Way (Mayu, the
  celestial river): Yacana the llama with her suckling calf, Atoq the fox, Hanp'atu the toad,
  Yutu the tinamou, Machacuay the serpent. The Milky Way's tilt divides the year; the ceque
  lines of Cusco run toward horizon risings.
- **Sources.** Polo de Ondegardo (1571), the Huarochiri manuscript, Garcilaso; modern
  ethnoastronomy (Urton at Misminay, 1981).
- **Atlas.** el_inca_garcilaso_de_la_vega, atahualpa; the Cusco ceque system is a network in
  the networks atlas already.

## 9. Polynesian and Pacific

- **Organizing principle.** A navigational sky: the star compass of Micronesia and Polynesia,
  thirty-two houses of the horizon by rising and setting points; star paths (kaveinga) followed
  in sequence through the night; Matariki (the Pleiades) opening the Maori year; Hokule'a
  (Arcturus) as Hawai'i's zenith star. Figures are few and functional: Maui's fishhook
  (Scorpius), the canoe.
- **Atlas.** none built.

## 10. Aboriginal Australian

- **Organizing principle.** The oldest continuous tradition; both star figures and dark
  constellations: the Emu in the Sky drawn by the dust lanes from the Coalsack along the Milky
  Way, whose posture through the year says when the eggs are laid; the Seven Sisters (Pleiades)
  pursued across the sky; Baiame's footprint; the Southern Cross as a stingray or a possum in a
  tree. Hundreds of language groups, no single system.
- **Atlas.** none built.

## 11. Norse, Sami and northern Europe

- Fragmentary: Thiassi's eyes (Castor and Pollux, or two stars of Gemini), Aurvandil's toe,
  Frigg's distaff (Orion's belt), the Wagon; the Sami have a hunting sky (the elk Sarva, the
  hunters, the bow) covering Cassiopeia, Perseus, Auriga and Taurus. Not a catalogue; a set of
  stories attached to a few bright groups.
- **Atlas.** leif_erikson, san_brendan carry the navigational sky in passing.

## 12. The early modern additions (Bayer to Lacaille)

- **Sources and counts.** Keyser and de Houtman's southern voyages (1595-97) yield twelve new
  southern constellations first printed on Plancius's globe (1598) and in Bayer's Uranometria
  (1603), which also fixed the Greek-letter naming of stars. Hevelius, Firmamentum Sobiescianum
  (1690): seven (or ten) new northern figures, among them Lacerta, Leo Minor, Lynx, Scutum,
  Sextans, Vulpecula, Canes Venatici. Lacaille (1751-52 at the Cape, published 1756): fourteen
  southern constellations named for instruments (Antlia, Fornax, Microscopium, Telescopium and
  the rest) and the cutting of Argo Navis into Carina, Puppis and Vela. Flamsteed's Atlas
  Coelestis (1729).
- **Atlas.** edmond_halley (his own southern catalogue, 1679, and a constellation for Charles
  II, Robur Carolinum, that did not survive), galileo_galilei; Bayer, Hevelius, Lacaille,
  Flamsteed, Plancius not built.

## 13. Christianized skies

- **Julius Schiller, Coelum Stellatum Christianum (Augsburg, 1627).** The whole pagan sky
  replaced: the twelve zodiac signs become the twelve apostles, the northern constellations New
  Testament figures, the southern ones Old Testament, Argo Navis becomes Noah's Ark. Complete,
  coherent, and dead within a generation, the purest hyperstition of the lot.
- Earlier attempts: Bede's and the medieval "Christian Aratus" glosses; Hildegard's own cosmos
  is a different thing, a vision of spheres, not a star map.
- **Atlas.** hildegard_von_bingen (the Scivias cosmos), noah (the Ark as Argo under Schiller);
  Julius Schiller not built (the roster's schiller is Friedrich, the poet: keep them apart).

## 14. Bode and the Enlightenment sky (the 1801 canon)

- **Sources.** Bode, Vorstellung der Gestirne (1782, 34 plates) and Uranographia (1801, 20
  plates, 17,240 stars, more than 100 constellations): the largest star atlas ever drawn with
  the figures over accurate positions, and the last. It carries the political and
  technological sky of its century: Honores Friderici, Sceptrum Brandenburgicum, Telescopium
  Herschelii, Globus Aerostaticus (the balloon), Machina Electrica, Officina Typographica (the
  printing press, 1801), Lochium Funis (the log line), Felis (the cat, Lalande's), Custos
  Messium, Quadrans Muralis (whose name survives in the Quadrantid meteors), Taurus Poniatovii,
  Turdus Solitarius, Harpa Georgii, Mons Maenalus, Cerberus, Musca Borealis, Triangulum Minus,
  Tarandus vel Rangifer, Robur Carolinum.
- **Atlas.** johann_bode (built, 44 stops). On chain: "Bode, Uranographia (the whole sky)",
  root 6e10058f..., 830 stars in 109 groups: the 88 modern constellations (Serpens in two halves,
  the second mislabeled Serpens Cauda), the Winter Triangle, and 19 of the dead ones above.

## 15. The IAU sky (1922 to 1930)

- **Sources.** The International Astronomical Union's first assembly (Rome, 1922) fixes the
  list of 88 constellations and their three-letter abbreviations; Eugène Delporte (Brussels)
  draws the boundaries along lines of right ascension and declination for epoch 1875 (published
  1930). Everything Bode's era added that is not in the 88 is abolished; the figures themselves
  are no longer defined, only the regions.
- **Atlas.** none built (Delporte not built).

## 16. Sky systems that are not constellations but shape the same space

- The Ptolemaic and Dantean spheres (the eighth sphere of the fixed stars, the ogdoad; dante,
  and the on-chain scene "Goethe's Southern Journey in the Dantean Cosmology", root 271ecf4d...,
  whose fixed-stars shell is the Bode quipu).
- The decans of Hellenistic and Renaissance magic (Picatrix, Agrippa; the Schifanoia frescoes).
- The Hermetic and Kabbalistic mappings of the zodiac onto letters (Sefer Yetzirah's twelve
  simple letters to the twelve signs; abraham_abulafia's permutations).

## Overview for inscription: each system as a celestial quipu like the Bode sky

The Bode collection on chain is one 0xce celestial quipu: 830 stars as RA/Dec points, 109 named
groups, lines inside each group. The same format takes any system whose figures are made of
stars; the dark-cloud skies need the trick noted. Star counts are what a faithful but sane
inscription would carry (the bright members that draw the figure), not the catalogue totals.

Reading the table: 3, 4, 14, 15, 16, 17 share most of their stars and differ in grouping; inscribed
in sequence they show the western sky being re-drawn six times over 1,500 years, which is the
collection's argument. 7 is the one genuinely independent large system and the biggest single
job. 10, 12 and 19 need the outline trick (points that are not stars); the 0xce format allows
it since a point is just RA/Dec with a name, but the titles should say so.

## What is inscribed, what is planned

- Inscribed: Bode's whole sky (109 groups), al-Sufi's Orion (10 groups), the Dantean scene that
  holds the Bode sky as its firmament.
- Planned (future_data_types.md, idea 2): constellations as their own data type, a fixed-sky
  position plus the myth in house style. Open: RA/Dec versus another convention; flat chart
  versus globe; prototypes versus all 88 plus Bode's dead ones.
- Mapmakers not yet subjects, if the systems are to be journeys: Ptolemy, Aratus, al-Sufi,
  Ulugh Beg, Bayer, Hevelius, Lacaille, Julius Schiller, Delporte.
