FIGURE. Robur Carolinum: invented by Edmond Halley in 1679 for Charles II, the Royal Oak of Boscobel, from stars of Argo Navis, on Bode's Uranographia 1801, abolished (already by Lacaille's rejection and the IAU). On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the oak in which Charles II hid after Worcester in 1651 (the Boscobel oak), placed by Halley in his Catalogus Stellarum Australium from St Helena to please the king, at the cost of Argo's stars, which Lacaille denounced; Halley's own atlas journey (his file exists: johann bode's contemporary? no: edmond_halley is a subject, cite); the Restoration sky with Cor Caroli; the stars back in Carina and Vela; the political constellations' fate.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
