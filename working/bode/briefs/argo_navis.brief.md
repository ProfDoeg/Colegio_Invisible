FIGURE. Argo Navis: one of Ptolemy's 48, drawn whole on Bode's Uranographia (1801), divided by Lacaille (1756) into Carina, Puppis and Vela (with Pyxis on its mast), the only Ptolemaic constellation abolished by the IAU (Delporte 1930); the 108th of the collection at Anthony's request, as the ship entire. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the ship of the Argonauts, built by Argus with Athena's help at Pagasae, with the speaking beam from the oak of Dodona, the first ship (Apollonius Argonautica, Apollodorus 1.9, Hyginus 2.37, Eratosthenes 35, Pindar Pyth. 4, Valerius Flaccus, Ovid); Jason, Medea, the Golden Fleece, the whole crew as a roster of catasterized heroes (Castor and Pollux, Heracles, Orpheus with his Lyra); the ship dedicated at Corinth or the Isthmus, the beam that killed Jason in his sleep; the ship shown stern-first in the sky, sailing backwards (Aratus 342 ff. and the ancient explanations: the Symplegades, the ship half-sunk); the Egyptian boat of Osiris and the Babylonian readings; the Bedouin Suhayl and the ship's stars in al-Sufi; Canopus the pilot of Menelaus; Bayer's plate and the single Greek-letter sequence that Lacaille's cut scattered across three constellations (why Vela and Puppis have no alpha or beta); Halley's Robur Carolinum carved from its stars (1679) and Lacaille's outrage; Lacaille's division in 1756 and the IAU's 1930 adoption of the pieces; Schiller's Noah's Ark; Dante's reading; the Argo of Catullus 64 and of Apollonius as the first ship and the loss of the golden age; Argo in the Renaissance emblem tradition (the Order of the Golden Fleece); the ship as a thing with a name, the oldest named object in the sky; where every star lives now.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
