FIGURE. Hydra: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Lernaean hydra of the second labor, or the water-snake of the Corvus and Crater story (Eratosthenes 41, Hyginus 2.40); the longest constellation; Alphard the solitary one; the Babylonian serpent MUSH (Ningishzida's beast) across the same stars; the Hydra as the celestial equator of 2800 BCE; the 'Sea of Cats' in Bedouin sky; Felis the cat once on its back (Lalande, abolished, separate dossier); Schiller's River Jordan; M83, the Ghost of Jupiter; TW Hydrae.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
