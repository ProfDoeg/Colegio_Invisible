FIGURE. Crux: the stars known to Ptolemy as part of Centaurus, separated as the Southern Cross by the navigators of the 1500s (Corsali 1515, Cadamosto, Vespucci), on Plancius's globe 1598 and Bayer 1603 as Crux, formalized by Royer 1679, kept by the IAU as the smallest constellation. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Cross of the Passion in the southern sky, Dante's four stars in Purgatorio 1 (written before any European saw them, the dispute over what he knew); the Coalsack and the Jewel Box; Acrux, Mimosa, Gacrux; the pointer to the pole; the Inca Chakana and the Andean cross; the Aboriginal stingray and possum; the Maori anchor; the flags of Brazil, Australia, New Zealand, Samoa, Papua New Guinea; Schiller's? (he made the whole Centaurus Abraham); the 'crux' of Amerigo Vespucci's letters.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
