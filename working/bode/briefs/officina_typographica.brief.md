FIGURE. Officina Typographica: invented by Bode in 1801 for the Uranographia, abolished by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the printing office, for the 350th anniversary of Gutenberg's press (Bode dates the invention 1450), drawn with a press, type cases and a printed sheet, east of Sirius between Canis Major and Monoceros in Puppis's stars; the press in the sky as Bode's monument to his own trade (he published his atlases himself); the Mochuelo resonance (a press among the stars, for a hand-press publisher); the stars' fate; Jamieson and Burritt keeping it into the 1830s.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
