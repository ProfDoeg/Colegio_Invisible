FIGURE. Serpens: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the serpent held by Ophiuchus, the only constellation in two parts, Caput and Cauda, since Delporte's 1930 boundaries; the serpent of Asclepius, the healing snake at Epidaurus (Pausanias 2.27), the serpent that brought the herb of resurrection (Hyginus 2.14); Unukalhai the serpent's neck; the Eagle Nebula M16 and the Pillars of Creation in Cauda; Hoag's Object; the Babylonian serpent readings; the Bode quipu's labeling of both halves as Cauda (a slip to note); Schiller's St Benedict's serpent; the brazen serpent of Moses in Christian readings.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
