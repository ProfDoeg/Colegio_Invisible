FIGURE. Aquila: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the eagle of Zeus, bearer of the thunderbolt, abductor of Ganymede (Eratosthenes 30, Hyginus 2.16, Ovid Met. 10), the eagle that ate Prometheus's liver; Altair the flying eagle, the Summer Triangle; Antinous, Hadrian's boy, carried in its talons from 132 CE to the IAU (Ptolemy's Antinous, Bode's plate); the Babylonian Eagle MUL.TI8.MUSHEN; the Chinese Cowherd Niulang and the Weaver across the river; Schiller's St Catherine; the Aquilid claims; the 389 CE nova; the first pulsar? (no: Vulpecula); the Vega-Altair legends.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
