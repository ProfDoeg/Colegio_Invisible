FIGURE. Ursa Major: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Callisto the Arcadian nymph turned bear by Hera or Artemis, nearly killed by her son Arcas, set in the sky by Zeus and forbidden to bathe in Ocean by Hera's plea to Tethys (Ovid Met. 2, Fasti 2, Hyginus 2.1, Eratosthenes 1); Helice the nurse of Zeus; the Wagon of Homer, Charles's Wain, the Plough, the Big Dipper, the Drinking Gourd of the Underground Railroad; Mizar and Alcor the horse and rider and the eyesight test, Mizar the first telescopic double (Riccioli 1650) and spectroscopic double (1889); the Seven Rishis of India, the Northern Dipper of China (the emperor's chariot), the Bedouin bier and the mourning daughters; Dubhe and Merak the Pointers; the Ursa Major moving group; M81, M82, the Owl, the Pinwheel; Schiller's Peter's Boat; the Bear in Homer, Job, and the Iroquois hunt; Bode's plate; the 'Great Bear' of Blake and Tennyson.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
