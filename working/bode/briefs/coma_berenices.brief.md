# Coma Berenices: research brief

FIGURE. Coma Berenices: an asterism named by Conon of Samos c. 245 BCE for the hair of Berenice II of Egypt, made a constellation by Vopel (1536) and Tycho Brahe's catalogue (1602), kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Berenice's Lock: the queen's hair vowed for Ptolemy III's return from the Syrian war, missing from the temple, found in the sky by Conon; Callimachus's Coma Berenices (Aetia fr. 110) and Catullus 66, its Latin translation, the lock that would rather be on her head; Ptolemy's 'nebulous mass' in Leo's tail; the Coma cluster of galaxies and Zwicky's dark matter (1933); the north galactic pole; Melotte 111; the Black Eye Galaxy; Schiller's Scourge of Christ? (verify); the Arabic 'the tuft'.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
