FIGURE. Honores Friderici: invented by Bode in 1787 (Friedrichs Ehre) from stars of Andromeda, Cassiopeia and Lacerta, in the Uranographia of 1801, abolished by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: a sword, pen and olive branch under a crown for Frederick the Great, who died in 1786, laid over Royer's Sceptrum et Manus Iustitiae (1679, for Louis XIV) and Hevelius's Lacerta; Bode's own text and plate; the Berlin Academy's sky; the rival royal constellations (Sceptrum Brandenburgicum, Taurus Poniatovii, Harpa Georgii, Robur Carolinum, Gladii Electorales Saxonici); its stars' fate in Andromeda and Lacerta; the sarcasm of later atlas-makers.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
