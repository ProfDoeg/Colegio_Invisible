FIGURE. Telescopium Herschelii: invented by Abbé Maximilian Hell in 1789 as two telescopes (Tubus Herschelii Major and Minor), one kept by Bode in 1801, abolished by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: William Herschel's 20-foot (or 40-foot) reflector, honoring the discovery of Uranus (1781), placed between Auriga, Lynx and Gemini where Uranus was found; Hell's two telescopes and Bode's one; Bode's own role in naming Uranus (his journey has it); Jamieson's plate; the stars in Auriga and Lynx now; the other telescope, Lacaille's Telescopium.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
