FIGURE. Ophiuchus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Serpent-bearer, Asclepius the healer, son of Apollo and Coronis, taught by Chiron, who raised the dead (Hippolytus, Glaucus) and was struck down by Zeus at Hades's complaint, set in the sky with his serpent (Eratosthenes 6, Hyginus 2.14, Ovid Met. 15), or Laocoön, or Phorbas, or Triopas; the Babylonian sitting god; Rasalhague; Barnard's Star; Kepler's supernova of 1604 in its foot; the rho Ophiuchi clouds; the thirteenth sign argument in full (the ecliptic crossing since Delporte 1930, the 1995 Mitton episode, 2011, 2016 NASA, the sidereal astrologers' answers); Schiller's St Benedict; the rod of Asclepius; the Chinese Heavenly Market.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
