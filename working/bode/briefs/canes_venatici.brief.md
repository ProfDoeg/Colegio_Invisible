FIGURE. Canes Venatici: introduced by Hevelius, Firmamentum Sobiescianum (1690), kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the hunting dogs Asterion and Chara held by Boötes, Hevelius's invention from stars Ptolemy left unformed, drawing on a misreading of Ptolemy's 'club' as a Greek word for dogs via Arabic (Allen's account, and Kunitzsch's correction); Cor Caroli, Charles I's or Charles II's heart named by Halley or Scarborough for the Restoration (1660s), one of the last royal star names to survive; the Whirlpool Galaxy M51 (Rosse 1845, the first spiral); M3; La Superba; the Hunting Dogs in Bode; Schiller's? (post-Schiller).

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
