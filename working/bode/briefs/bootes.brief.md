FIGURE. Boötes: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the ploughman or herdsman driving the Bears, Arctophylax; Icarius who received the vine from Dionysus and was killed by the shepherds, with Erigone (Virgo) and the dog Maera (Hyginus 2.4, Eratosthenes 8); Arcas son of Callisto; Philomelus inventor of the wagon; Arcturus the bear-guard, the brightest of the northern sky, in Job and Hesiod's Works and Days as a season marker, in the 1933 Chicago fair's opening; the Quadrans Muralis in its stars (Bode's abolished quadrant, the Quadrantid meteors); Schiller's St Sylvester.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
