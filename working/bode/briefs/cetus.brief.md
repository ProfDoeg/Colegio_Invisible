FIGURE. Cetus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the sea monster sent by Poseidon to devour Andromeda, slain by Perseus (Ovid Met. 4, Hyginus 2.31), the whale of Jonah in Schiller and in the sailors' sky; Mira the Wonderful, the first periodic variable (Fabricius 1596, Holwarda 1638), and Mira's tail; Deneb Kaitos, Menkar; Tau Ceti and Ozma; the Babylonian sea monster Tiamat readings; the Ketos of Joppa and its bones shown at Rome by Scaurus (Pliny 9.11); Bode's plate; the Cetus-Pisces border and the vernal point.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
