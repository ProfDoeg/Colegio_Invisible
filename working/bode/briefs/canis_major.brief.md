FIGURE. Canis Major: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Laelaps the dog that always caught its prey, given by Zeus to Europa, to Minos, to Procris, to Cephalus, turned to stone with the Teumessian fox (Ovid Met. 7, Hyginus 2.35), or Orion's dog; Sirius the Dog Star, the Egyptian Sopdet/Sothis and the Nile flood and the Sothic cycle, the Greek dog days and the sacrifices of red dogs, Homer's Iliad 22 (Achilles as the Dog Star); the color of Sirius controversy (Ptolemy's 'reddish'); the Dogon and the Sirius B claim (Temple 1976) and its refutation; Sirius B's discovery 1862; Schiller's St David; the Chinese Celestial Wolf; the star of Isis at Dendera.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
