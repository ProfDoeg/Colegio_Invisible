FIGURE. Custos Messium: invented by Lalande in 1775 for Charles Messier, on Bode's Uranographia 1801, abolished by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the Harvest Keeper, a pun on Messier's name (messis, harvest), a farmer with a sickle beside Camelopardalis and Cassiopeia, near the reindeer (Tarandus) and the 'harvest' of the Great Comet of 1774; Messier's catalogue and his comet-hunting; the stars' fate in Cassiopeia, Cepheus and Camelopardalis; the French astronomers naming each other in the sky (Lalande's Felis, Messier's Custos).

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
