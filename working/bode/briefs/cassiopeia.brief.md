FIGURE. Cassiopeia: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the boasting queen of Ethiopia, mother of Andromeda, who claimed to outshine the Nereids, set in the sky upside down in her chair as punishment (Eratosthenes 16, Hyginus 2.10, Ovid Met. 4); the W and the Chair; Tycho's supernova of 1572 in it, De nova stella, the death of the Aristotelian sky; Cassiopeia A the radio source and the unseen supernova of c. 1680 (Flamsteed's 3 Cas?); Schedar, Caph, the Bedouin 'tinted hand'; the Arabic Dhat al-Kursi the lady in the chair; Schiller's Mary Magdalene; the Chinese Wang Liang.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
