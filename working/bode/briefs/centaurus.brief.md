FIGURE. Centaurus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Chiron the wise centaur, teacher of Achilles, Jason, Asclepius, wounded by Heracles's arrow and set in the sky (Eratosthenes 40, Hyginus 2.38), or Pholus; the centaur holding the Beast (Lupus) toward the altar; Alpha Centauri and Proxima, the nearest stars, Rigil Kentaurus, Hadar; Omega Centauri the great cluster, Halley 1677; Centaurus A the radio galaxy; the Southern Cross once its hind legs (Ptolemy), cut out by Plancius and Bayer; the Bedouin sky; Schiller's Abraham and Isaac; the 1006 supernova in Lupus next door.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
