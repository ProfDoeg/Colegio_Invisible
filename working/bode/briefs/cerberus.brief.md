Cerberus: invented by Hevelius in 1687-90 as the three-headed dog (drawn as a three-headed snake) in Hercules's hand, on Bode's Uranographia 1801 (Cerberus et Ramus Pomifer, with the apple branch), abolished by the IAU in 1922. on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the hound of Hades, Heracles's twelfth labor (Apollodorus 2.5, Hesiod Theog.), Hevelius's figure as three serpents held by Hercules; Bode's addition of the apple branch (Ramus Pomifer) of the Hesperides; the stars in Hercules now; the Cerberus of Dante and the myth's whole file; Jamieson's plate.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
