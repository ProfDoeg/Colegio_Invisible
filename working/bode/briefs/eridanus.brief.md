FIGURE. Eridanus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the river into which Phaethon fell, the Po or the Rhône or the Nile, where Cycnus mourned and the Heliades wept amber (Ovid Met. 2, Hyginus 2.32, Eratosthenes 37); Ptolemy's Potamos; the Babylonian river; Achernar 'the end of the river', the second brightest of the south, unseen by Ptolemy and added by Bayer; Cursa, Zaurak; Epsilon Eridani and the Ozma search; Schiller's Red Sea crossing; the Bedouin ostriches; the Egyptian Nile reading; the star-of-Eudoxus dispute (Ptolemy's Eridanus ended at Acamar).

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
