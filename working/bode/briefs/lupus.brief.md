FIGURE. Lupus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Ptolemy's Therion the Beast, held by the Centaur on a pole toward the Altar, a wolf only from the Renaissance; Lycaon the Arcadian king turned wolf by Zeus (Ovid Met. 1) as a late identification; the Babylonian Mad Dog UR.IDIM in the same stars; the supernova of 1006, the brightest in recorded history, seen from St Gall to Cairo (Ali ibn Ridwan) to China; Schiller's Benjamin; the Bedouin sky; no named bright star, Men.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
