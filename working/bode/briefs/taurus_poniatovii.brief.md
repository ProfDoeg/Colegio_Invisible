FIGURE. Taurus Poniatovii: invented by Marcin Poczobut in 1777 for King Stanisław August Poniatowski of Poland, on Bode's Uranographia 1801, abolished by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: Poniatowski's Bull, in stars between Ophiuchus, Serpens and Aquila (the V-shaped asterism like the Hyades, 66-70 Ophiuchi), created by the Vilnius astronomer for the last king of Poland, the Poniatowski arms' bull; 70 Ophiuchi the binary and the 1855 planet claim (Jacob); Barnard's Star nearby; the Polish sky with Scutum Sobiescianum; the partitions of Poland and the constellation's death.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
