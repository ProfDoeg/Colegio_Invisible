FIGURE. Delphinus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the dolphin that found Amphitrite for Poseidon (Eratosthenes 31, Hyginus 2.17), the dolphin that saved Arion of Lesbos (Herodotus 1.23-24, Hyginus 2.17); Job's Coffin; Sualocin and Rotanev, Niccolò Cacciatore's name spelled backwards on the Palermo catalogue of 1814, the only stars named for a living assistant by a trick; the Bedouin camel; Schiller's Water Jar of Cana; the 1967 nova HR Delphini.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
