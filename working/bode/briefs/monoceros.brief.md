FIGURE. Monoceros: introduced by Plancius on his 1613 globe (Monoceros Unicornis), adopted by Bartsch 1624 and Hevelius, kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the unicorn, from the Physiologus and the Bible's re'em, placed by Plancius in the gap between Orion's dogs; the Rosette Nebula and the Christmas Tree cluster; Plaskett's Star; Beta Monocerotis the triple (Herschel's 'most beautiful sight'); the Cone Nebula; the unicorn tapestries as the iconography; Bode's plate; the dispute over a 1564 Persian-sphere mention.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
