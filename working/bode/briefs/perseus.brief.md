FIGURE. Perseus: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the hero who cut off Medusa's head with Athena's shield and Hermes's sandals, saved Andromeda, turned Polydectes to stone (Apollodorus 2.4, Ovid Met. 4-5, Hyginus 2.12, Eratosthenes 22); Algol the Demon's Head, the winking star, Goodricke's 1782 eclipse explanation, the Egyptian Calendar of Lucky and Unlucky Days claim (2012); Mirfak; the Double Cluster (Hipparchus's 'cloudy spot'); the Perseids from Swift-Tuttle, the tears of St Lawrence; the Alpha Persei cluster; the California Nebula; Schiller's St Paul; the Chinese Tianchuan the celestial boat; the Bedouin 'bearer of the demon's head' Hamil Ra's al-Ghul.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
