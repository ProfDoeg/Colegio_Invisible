FIGURE. Carina: introduced by Lacaille from the Cape of Good Hope (1751-52, published 1756), kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the keel of Argo Navis, Jason's ship, cut by Lacaille into Carina, Puppis and Vela (with Pyxis as the compass), keeping Bayer's letters divided among the pieces; the Argonauts' whole myth (Apollonius, Hyginus 2.37, Eratosthenes 35: the ship built by Argus with Athena's help, the speaking beam of Dodona, the first ship), the ship in the sky shown stern-first; Canopus the pilot of Menelaus buried at the Egyptian town, the second brightest star, the Bedouin Suhayl; Eta Carinae and the Great Eruption of 1837-43 (Herschel at the Cape), the Homunculus, the Keyhole Nebula; the Diamond Cross and the False Cross; Schiller's Noah's Ark for the whole Argo; the Chinese Old Man of the South Pole (Canopus).

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
