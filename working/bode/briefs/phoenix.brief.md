FIGURE. Phoenix: one of the twelve southern constellations of Keyser and de Houtman, first on Plancius's globe of 1598 and in Bayer's Uranometria (1603), kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the phoenix of Heliopolis, Herodotus 2.73, Ovid Met. 15, Pliny 10.2, the Physiologus, the bennu bird of Egypt, Lactantius's De ave phoenice; a Dutch constellation given the Greek bird's whole mythology after the fact; the Arabic 'the boat' and the ostrich chicks in the same stars; Ankaa; the Phoenicids; the Sculptor galaxy border; Schiller's? (the Bayer twelve in Schiller).

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
