FIGURE. Orion: one of Ptolemy's 48 (Almagest, c. 150 CE), kept by the IAU in 1922. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the giant hunter, son of Poseidon or born from the bull's hide (Hyginus 2.34, Eratosthenes 32), Merope and Oenopion's blinding, Eos, the scorpion sent by Gaia or Artemis, Artemis's arrow and the sea; Homer's Iliad 18 and Odyssey 5 and 11; Hesiod's harvest markers; the Babylonian True Shepherd of Anu (SIPA.ZI.AN.NA) and Gilgamesh readings; the Egyptian Sah as Osiris, the pyramid-alignment claim (Bauval) and its critics; al-Sufi's al-Jawza (on chain already, cite it); Betelgeuse's 2019-20 dimming and its supernova watch, Rigel, Bellatrix, the Belt (the Three Kings, the Three Marys), the Sword and M42 (Huygens 1656), the Horsehead, Barnard's Loop; the Orionids from Halley's comet; Schiller's St Joseph; the Chinese Shen mansion; Orion in Job and Amos; the Aboriginal Julpan canoe; the Maya hearthstones.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
