FIGURE. Camelopardalis: introduced by Plancius on his 1612-13 globe, published by Bartsch 1624, kept by the IAU. On Johann Elert Bode's Uranographia (1801) and in the Bode collection
on chain (830 stars in 109 groups). Give its status plainly at the top: kept by the IAU with its
1930 boundaries, or abolished, and where its stars went.

THREADS TO PULL, each to be verified against the sources and dated: the giraffe (camel-leopard), filling the empty space between the Bears, Cassiopeia and Auriga; Bartsch's gloss that it was the camel that carried Rebecca to Isaac (Genesis 24) and the later Christian readings; the faintness of the figure, no star above fourth magnitude; Kemble's Cascade; NGC 2403; the runaway star Alpha Camelopardalis; the Chinese Purple Enclosure officials in the same stars; Custos Messium and Tarandus once laid over its stars (separate dossiers); the confusion of 'camelopardalis' with 'camel' in atlases; the IAU's 1930 giant boundary.

STANCE. Myth as fully as fact. Every claim labeled: documented fact, scholarly reconstruction,
tradition, disputed, legend, modern invention; the first appearance of each story, who spread it,
and what evidence exists. Where a thread above is marked '(verify)' or '(no: ...)', treat it as a
lead to check, not a fact, and report what you found. Absence of evidence is a finding: say what
could not be traced. For an abolished figure, everything that survives about it, however little.
